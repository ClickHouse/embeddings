# QBit representation recall benchmark

Recall of the QBit transposed-distance approximations vs exact full-precision cosine,
across all three embeddings and every (type × rotation × bits × dims) combination.

**Method:** per dataset, a 100k-row random sample (`bench.samp_*`), 20 random query points,
ground truth = exact top-100 by full-precision cosine over the raw `embedding`. For each config,
the approximate top-100 is computed with `cosineDistanceTransposed` (BFloat16) /
`cosineDistanceTransposedQuantized` (Int8) at the given bits/dims, averaged over the 20 queries.
Metrics: **Recall@10** (top-10 ∩ top-10), **Recall@100** (top-100 ∩ top-100),
**Recall 10-in-100** (true top-10 found within approx top-100). Raw data: `recall.csv`.
Reproduce: `scripts/recall_bench.py` (+ the `bench.*` sample/query/gt tables).

## Key findings

- **1–2-bit BFloat16 is useless (recall ≈ 0).** The top BFloat16 bit-planes are the sign and high
  exponent bits, which don't order magnitudes. BFloat16 also has a pathological **dip at 7–8 bits**
  (sign + exponent, ~no mantissa) before recovering at 9+ bits — so BFloat16 bit-truncation is *not* graceful.
- **Int8 degrades gracefully and dominates at low bit budgets.** Int8 recall is monotonic in bits;
  Int8@1-bit ≈ BFloat16@3-bit, and Int8 reaches ~0.9 by 4–5 bits at full dims. For equal bits (≤8), Int8 ≥ BFloat16.
- **Rotated ≥ original**, most at low bits/dims (the Hadamard rotation spreads information evenly), and
  rotated exposes more dims (siglip2 2048 vs 1152), so rotated at max dims reaches 1.00.
- **Recall rises monotonically with dims**; full bits + full dims → 1.00 (sanity check passes).
- **Sweet spots:** Int8 · rotated · 4-bit · full-dims ≈ 0.94–0.96 Recall@100 (half the bytes of 8-bit);
  Int8 · rotated · 8-bit · full ≈ 0.99–1.00. The explorer default (Int8/rotated/1-bit/128d) ≈ 0.26 — a deliberately coarse, fast first pass.
